You don’t build a club on the city’s industrial edge and hope for footfall. In fact you probably shouldn’t expect a venue in the vast complex of Meridian Water to prove a cash cow anytime soon.

Not only is there no way customers will ever walk past this place by chance, they’ll need to reassure their Uber drivers they’re not taking them to an industrial estate in the early hours of the morning for an arms trafficking meetup.

But what this does mean, is that if you build a club here you probably did it out of a love for clubbing itself. That’s the vibe you get from the work Unit H have done, the team behind this particular club, aptly named Gaffe.

Gaffe immediately feels refreshing. Like its name it's a comfortable, unpretentious spot that seems to have attracted a crowd of a similar ethos.

We found a bubbly queue at an innocuous warehouse entrance with a security team that was as friendly as it was thorough: ID scanned, pockets patted, a smile on the way through.

After a canyon of warehouses it takes a moment to adjust before the layout makes sense. A straight-up pitch-black dance floor, bar a few pulsing strobes, a kind of chill-out room with sofas, and a smoking area. What more could you need?

Not much else it turns out. Coats stay on for the foreseeable. There’s space enough to move and the temperature is low enough to justify it. It won't last, but it'll take a moment to get there.

Opener Ploy was using the Deaf Tech club night to launch his new label of the same name and he played like someone with something to celebrate. With a background in dubstep and drum and bass his love of instant-gratification tech-house was all over the set with a drop every minute and intoxicating basslines and grooves that invited you in straight away. The crowd answered with giraffe arms counting the beat and dropped-shoulder two-stepping. At this point we decided we could use the cloak room and remove our restrictive puffers.

Nights like this show how far tech-house has come. From its minimal beginnings, through a fairly drab spell courting big-room commercial crowds, it has grown into something sharper and more playful again and Ploy’s set is a good advert for it.

Towards 2am headliner Batu stepped up for a never-seen-before tech-house set. The ‘Your DJ’s favourite DJ’ tag enshrined on him because of his eclectic sets and dexterous mixing. He treated the tech house challenge the same way as somewhere to explore. He tucked into deeper, minimal beats and lifted the tempo only occasionally, weaving the room in with him rather than telling it how to behave a la tech house standard format. Stripped-back percussive pieces sat next to Martinez Brothers tracks and the joins never showed.

The Funktion 1 sound system deserves its own paragraph. As stated, you build a club this far out because you care how a kick drum lands in a dark room. On this evidence, Gaffe should earn the kind of reputation destination clubs like FOLD has in Canning Town or Venue MOT south of the river. The sound system punched delicately, for a fun tech house set it was perfect, geeing up the crowd and adding the clarity that makes you lose yourself in dance.

Emerging South Korean artist Cosmo took the room into the later morning with older-school techno and tech-house. Davi L Dj’s decade-old ‘Bot Bot Help’ gave the closing hours a more traditional morning rave air.

The real standout, though, was the crowd. They were there to really dance. At a time when clubs are pushing back on chatter and phones on the dancefloor, nobody here needed reminding and that’s a refreshing antidote.

Gaffe feels like a club that will quickly become a destination: crate-like walls around a pitch-black floor made for losing yourself, and plenty of personality from venue and crowd alike.

Well worth reassuring your cab driver for, we can’t wait to return.
